# Five Reels concepts for the PastForward account

## What this is built on

**The control**, live since 14 Nov, the only ad in the account:

> **Make your room yours**
> Your favourite albums, made for your wall. Posters, vinyl and polaroids from ₹99.
> `SHOP_NOW`

5.27% CTR · 4.12% website CTR · ₹2.73 CPC · 113 landing page views · 14 add to cart · 0 purchases.

**What the control teaches.** It leads with the room and the person, not the product, and it names a price floor. The click rate is well above typical ecommerce prospecting, so the hook works. Everything after the click is the problem, which is why these concepts keep the identity framing and vary what gets promised.

**Grounding limit, stated plainly.** There are no reviews, no ad comments and no customer quotes, which are normally the highest-value inputs for this work. These five are grounded in the account's own performance, the brand voice rules and the catalogue. They are hypotheses, not proven angles. Refresh this file once real orders produce real language.

## Hard constraints

- **No recognisable album art as the lead visual.** Third-party cover art and artist names in paid creative risk rejection or an IP report. Lead with the object, the wall, the room and custom. Album art may appear incidentally and out of focus, never as the subject.
- **Instagram Reels only matters.** 73% of spend, 97% of clicks, cheapest CPM on the account. Shoot 9:16. Keep text inside the 720×1200 safe band.
- Brand voice: plain, specific, physical, faintly wry. No exclamation marks. No emoji. Prices stated flatly. Nothing a candle brand could paste onto their own site.

## A — The object

> **A record that had a life first** (30)
> Real 12-inch vinyl, cleaned up, with your album set into the centre label. Made to hang. Wall hook in the box. ₹999. (116)
> `Free delivery across India` (26)

**Visual (9:16):** Hands turning a record over, raking light across the grooves. Close enough to see the shine and the dust. The centre label stays out of focus or carries the PastForward mark.

**Why:** The upcycled record is the one thing no competitor has, and the control never mentions it.

**Hypothesis:** Lower CTR than the control, higher landing-page-view → ViewContent rate, because it filters for people who want the object rather than the aesthetic.

## B — Custom

> **Name any album. We'll make it.** (30)
> The album that actually means something to you probably isn't in anyone's catalogue. Send it to us. Same price, ₹999. (117)
> `Made to order in India` (22)

**Visual (9:16):** A phone typing an album name into the custom form, cut to a blank record, cut to the finished piece going up on a wall. Nothing recognisable on camera.

**Why:** The archive proves custom orders already happen, and it is the brand's strongest differentiator. It also sidesteps IP entirely.

**Hypothesis:** Lowest CTR of the five, highest add-to-cart rate per click.

## C — The wall

> **Three records, one wall** (23)
> One record looks good. Three in a row turns a blank wall into the reason people stop walking. Any three, ₹2,499. (112)
> `Save ₹498` (9)

**Visual (9:16):** A bare wall, one record up, then three. Shot in a real room, not a studio.

**Why:** Makes the ₹2,499 bundle real and raises order value, which matters because Purchase optimisation needs volume the current AOV can't fund.

**Hypothesis:** Lower CTR, higher average order value. Judge on revenue per click, not CTR.

## D — Entry price

> **Start a wall for ₹99** (20)
> Polaroid cards are ₹99. A5 posters are ₹199. Put ten up around a mirror and the room stops looking like a rental. (113)
> `Free delivery, 7–10 days` (24)

**Visual (9:16):** Hands sticking polaroid cards around a mirror and over a desk. Fast, cheap, tactile.

**Why:** The control already promises "from ₹99" and the landing page now honours it. This makes the whole ad about the cheap way in.

**Hypothesis:** Highest CTR of the five and the first to produce a purchase, at the lowest order value.

## E — Gift

> **For the one who sends you songs** (31)
> They've had the same album on repeat since school. Put it on their wall instead. From ₹99, delivered anywhere in India. (119)
> `Send it straight to them` (24)

**Visual (9:16):** Unboxing in someone's hands, their reaction off camera. A real room.

**Why:** The context doc names the gifter as a distinct buyer, and nothing in the account speaks to them.

**Hypothesis:** Beats the control on CTR in the 25–35 band and underperforms it under 22.

## How to run these

Add them as additional ads in the existing ad set rather than new ad sets. One ad set keeps the conversion signal pooled, and the account has nowhere near enough volume to split it. The control stays live as the benchmark.

Give each 1,000+ impressions before judging. On this budget that is roughly a day each, so the slate needs about a week to read.

**Judge on cost per add-to-cart, not CTR.** The control already proves a high CTR does not produce sales here.
